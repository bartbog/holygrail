> Low-level or otherwise minor comments
> -------------------------------------

> This paper is specific to explaining puzzles, or at least CSPs with a
> unique solution. Therefore, the title is at least slightly misleading.

> I do not understand why the _Split variant is faster. According to the
> explanation provided in section 6.2, it should replicate what the MIP
> solver already does. What have I misunderstood? What was the intuition
> for introducing this variant?

It is true that the _Split variant mimics to some extent what the MIP solver already does.

However, _Split improves the efficiency of the computation in several ways:
	- First OUS is highly dependent on the ordering of the literals to explain. For instance, if the first few literals are very costly to explain, then the focus of computation will be completely on the wrong literals while the cheaper literals are not yet explored.
	- Reducing the input unsatisfiable formula for each literal implies the computed subsets only contain relevant information (hitting sets and corrections) regarding the literal to explain e.g. no negations of other literals to explain. 
	- For instance, the CorrSubsets procedure needs to reason over less information, meaning less time is spent in grow and giving smaller and better corrections only relevant to the literal explained.
	- Every iteration in the OCUS algorithm increases the number of sets to hit and makes the finding an optimal hitting harder. 
	- _SPlit doesn't require adding the exactly one constraint  to the MIP solver.
	- Finally, only the most promising literal is expanded, other literals with a higher cost hitting set are pushed to the back of the priority queue.
