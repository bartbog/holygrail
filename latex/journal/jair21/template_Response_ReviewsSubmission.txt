> Reviewer B:
> 
> The story of the paper is that existing certification techniques
> (mostly DRAT, really) are inadequate to express symmetry (and
> dominance) breaking, and therefore a new approach is needed, and this
> new approach should be based on PB formulas and cutting planes. My
> main questions are: 
> 1. Can you explain in more detail why DRAT cannot do what your system
> can? Maybe give at least a small example? Is it intractable to express
> symmetry breaking in DRAT? Is it merely impractical?

It is unknown whether general symmetry breaking can be done
efficiently using the DRAT proof system (as we try to explain already
in the introduction of the paper). To the best of our knowledge, there
is no method (impractical or not) for fully general symmetry breaking
using DRAT. Proving that DRAT *cannot* do this, however, would require
monumental breakthroughs in computational complexity theory, since it
would simultaneously establish impossibility results that seem
completely beyond reach given the current state of the art.

We have added a discussion about this.

> 2. Your redundance and dominance rule are inspired by the propagation
> redundancy (PR) SAT proof system. It seems that PR should readily be
> able to express the SAT equivalent of your redundance rule. Is it true
> that it cannot express the SAT equivalent of your dominance rule? What
> would even be the SAT equivalent of the dominance rule? Can you do a
> SAT (clausal) version of your proof system (perhaps based on PR), or
> is it essential to use cutting planes?

Yes, the redundance rule works also in clausal proof systems, and is quite
similar to rules that have been suggested for proof systems extending DRAT.

It is not so clear how to implement the dominance rule in a strictly
clausal proof system, however. In our proof system, we crucially use that
with 0-1 linear inequalities we can reason about potential functions and
how such functions decrease, and can use this to obtain an order which we
can reason about inside the proof system. The fact that we have
inequalities in the proof system makes this really convenient. Implementing
this in strictly clausal form would be more complicated, since it would
require us to prove that sets of clauses encode and enforce the type of
order relations that we need (and this would also involve taking care of
extension variables, which would be necessitated by the clausal format).


> 3. Clearly, the redundance and dominance rule are similar. Can you
> provide some intuitive explanation where the difference lies? In
> particular, can you p-simulate one with the other, and if not can you
> prove a separation? 

We have not studied this in depth, but the question of how cutting planes
with redundance-based strengthening on the one hand and cutting planes with
dominance-based strengthening on the other are related seems quite
nontrivial. In particular, it seems quite conveivable that cutting planes
with dominance *cannot* be polynomially simulated by cutting planes with
redundance, but proving such a result would involve major research
breakthroughs in computational complexity theory.

We now mention this open question in the conclusion.
