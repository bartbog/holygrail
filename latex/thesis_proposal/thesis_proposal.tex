This document gives an overview of the research activities that I’m planning for the remainder of my joint PhD. The research activities reflect my current interest in how to explain constraint satisfaction and optimisation problems. In the first activity, I will investigate how to efficiently compute explanations under uncertainty The second activity is related to optimizing explanation sequences for constraint satisfaction problems. Next, we build on the results of the previous activity, to provide methods for abstracting and refining the explanation sequence.
Finally, the last activity will be devoted to writing the PhD dissertation and preparing for the oral defence.
\section{Research Plan}
\subsection{Research activity 1: \emph{Explaining constraint solving under uncertainty}}
\paragraph{Role}Main author
\paragraph{Target Period} July 2022 – February 2023
\paragraph{Target}AAAI-23 (conference paper)
\paragraph{Collaborations}Maxime Mulamba (VUB-KULeuven), Bart Bogaerts (VUB), Tias Guns (KULeuven)

\paragraph{Context} In a real-life context, many combinatorial problems, such as Sudoku puzzles, come under the form of an image. Such problems require the integration of perception, using, for instance Machine Learning (ML), and a reasoning task to solve a combinatorial problem from visual input. 
Using the techniques for integrating ML and Constraint Solving developed by PhD researcher Maxime Mulambda, we investigate how to integrate the cost-optimal explanation techniques we have developed. 
The goal is to provide explanations under uncertainty where the given input are probabilistic estimates of the prediction task.

\subsection{Research activity 2: \emph{Optimizing explanation sequences}}
\paragraph{Role}Main author
\paragraph{Target Period}November 2022 – May 2023
\paragraph{Target}IJCAI/CP-23 (conference paper)
\paragraph{Collaborations}Bart Bogaerts (VUB), Tias Guns (KULeuven)

\paragraph{Context} In previous works, I, Tias Guns and Bart Bogaerts have studied how to generate a sequence of simple explanations, where difficulty of an explanation is quantified using a cost function. The focus of that work has been on generating these explanations efficiently and guarantee that they are cost-optimal with respect to the cost-function. While the focus is on generating these explanations independently, in this research activity the goal is to provide better sequences of explanations. 
Therefore, in this research activity, we investigate alternative measures of complexity of an explanation sequence, considering for instance that a sequence is easier if there is a relation between subsequent steps to avoid constant context switches which make the reasoning harder to understand for humans. The goal is to develop generic (with respect to the complexity measure used) global algorithms building on previous work to provide an optimal explanation sequence.

\subsection{Research activity 3: \emph{Abstraction – Refinement of explanation sequences}}
\paragraph{Role}Main author
\paragraph{Target Period} April 2023 – September 2023
\paragraph{Target}AAAI 24 (conference paper)
\paragraph{Collaborations}Bart Bogaerts (VUB), Tias Guns (KULeuven)


\paragraph{Context} The explanation sequences generated to explain how to solve constraint satisfaction problems are highly dependent on the way the problem is modelled and encoded into the solver. For instance, the Sudoku problem can be modelled using only integer values, where an explanation step corresponds to deriving a new number to fill in the grid. However, the same sudoku problem can also be encoded into a Boolean problem where an explanation step can also correspond to deriving a number that cannot be taken. 
Using the complexity measures developed for optimizing explanation sequences in Research Activity 2, we investigate how to zoom-in or abstract parts of the explanation sequence.
